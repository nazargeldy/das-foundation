Programmes

Three strands that hold each other up.

Collection without education fills bins with contaminated material. Education without collection is a lecture. We run both, plus the piece that makes recovered material worth something.

Programme 01

Community recovery points

Reliable neighbourhood drop-off for recyclable material.

A recovery point is a staffed or semi-staffed drop-off location with a published collection schedule, clearly labelled separation for each accepted material, and a named local coordinator. Nothing about it is technically sophisticated. What makes it work is that it is there on the day it says it will be.

What it involves

  • A site audit to establish material volumes and identify buyers within viable transport distance
  • Separation bins and signage specific to the materials that local processors will actually take
  • A fixed, published collection schedule and a local coordinator trained to run it
  • Simple record-keeping so weights collected can be reported back to the community

What we need from a host community

Somewhere secure to place the point, a local group or individual willing to coordinate, and agreement from whoever owns or governs the land. We handle the audit, the setup, the training and the processor relationships.

Programme 02

Schools & youth

A waste audit students run themselves, then act on.

Environmental education fails when it stays abstract. Our schools programme starts with students collecting, sorting and weighing one week of their own school's waste. The numbers are theirs, the mess is theirs, and the conclusions are considerably harder to ignore than a poster.

How it runs

  • Week one — audit. Students sort and weigh a week of school waste by material category and record the results.
  • Week two — analysis. They work out what fraction was recoverable, and where it was being lost.
  • Week three — design. Student teams propose a change to the school's own system: bin placement, signage, canteen packaging, a collection routine.
  • Week four — implement and re-measure. The school adopts a proposal, and students repeat the audit to see whether it moved.

Suitable for

Upper primary through secondary, adaptable to term length. We provide the materials, the audit framework and facilitation; the school provides class time and permission to change something.

Programme 03

Upcycling & livelihoods

Making recovered material worth handling.

Some materials have no local buyer. Collecting them anyway just moves a storage problem around. This programme trains people in repair and upcycling techniques that turn those materials into goods with local value — so recovery produces income rather than a growing pile.

What it covers

  • Practical skills training in repair, textile reuse and material fabrication
  • Basic costing and pricing, so participants can tell which items are worth their time
  • Connections to local markets, retailers and buyers
  • Follow-up sessions after the initial training, which is where most such programmes quietly stop

Who it is for

Priority goes to people already working in waste recovery informally, and to communities hosting a recovery point where a share of collected material has no processor.
