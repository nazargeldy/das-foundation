A greener planet through action.

Das Foundation works where waste is actually created and thrown away — in homes, schools and small businesses. We build the collection routes, the sorting habits and the practical knowledge that keep recoverable material out of landfill and waterways.

The problem

Most waste is recyclable. Almost none of it gets recycled.

The barrier is rarely willingness. People are asked to separate materials without being told which ones are actually accepted, with no bin that fits their kitchen, and no collection that reliably turns up. So it all goes into one bag, and a stream of perfectly recoverable glass, aluminium, paper and PET goes to landfill.

We treat that as a logistics and education problem rather than a moral one. Fix the route, make the sorting obvious, show people where their material ends up, and participation follows — and it holds, because the system stopped asking for heroism.

What we do

Three programmes, one chain.

Each programme feeds the next. Material has to be collected, people have to know how to sort it, and someone has to make it worth doing.

01

Community recovery points

Neighbourhood drop-off points with a fixed, published collection schedule and clear signage for every accepted material. Predictability is the whole product — a route people can plan around.

02

Schools & youth programme

Waste audit workshops that have students weigh and sort what their own school throws away for a week, then design the fix. The lesson lands because the rubbish is theirs.

03

Upcycling & livelihoods

Training in repair and upcycling for materials with no local recycling market, so that recovered material becomes income rather than a storage problem.

How it works

How a new site comes together.

We do not arrive with a container and a logo. A recovery point only works if the people using it helped place it.

  1. Listen first

    We meet residents, barangay or council officials and local waste workers to map what is already happening. Informal collectors usually know the material flows better than anyone.

  2. Map the material

    A short audit establishes what is actually thrown away, in what volume, and which of it has a buyer within reasonable distance.

  3. Build the route

    Drop-off points go where people already walk. We publish the schedule and commit to it — a missed collection costs more trust than a month of outreach earns.

  4. Hand it over

    Every site is designed to be run locally. We stay involved for training and reporting, but the aim is a programme that no longer needs us.

Common questions

Before you get in touch.

Which materials do you accept?

This varies by site, because it depends entirely on which processors operate nearby. Every recovery point publishes its own accepted-materials list on site. Contact us for the list covering your area.

Can you set up a collection point in my neighbourhood?

Possibly. The deciding factors are whether there is a local group willing to help run it and whether there is a processor within viable distance for the materials involved. Get in touch and we will walk through both with you.

Do you work with schools?

Yes — the schools programme is one of our three core strands. It runs as a waste audit followed by student-designed interventions, and we adapt it to the length of term and age group.

Are you asking for donations?

Not through this website. If you want to support the work, the most useful things you can offer are time, transport, storage space, or an introduction to a processor.

Change that matters starts locally.

Whether you want to volunteer, bring the programme to your school, or partner with us as a business — we would like to hear from you.
